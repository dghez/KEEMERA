Plate V · Of the hand that scatters

Letters flee the hand.

Litterae manum fugiunt.

Pass the cursor over the columns. Each gives way about its own centre, by letter, by word or by line, and the farther it is driven the deeper it turns to rubric.

Centres
Letter, word and line
The hand

CPU

I · By the letter

The letters part like water about the hand, and close again once it has passed. Each is its own small piece, pushed from the centre of its ink.

The outline is read from the letter’s own coordinates, so a letter driven far from home is still drawn sharp.

Nothing is reckoned on the CPU: the hand is the store’s own mouse, eased, and shared with the shader as it stands.

II · By the word

Each word holds together and travels as a single piece, as words should, measured from the middle of the word.

Long words, like illumination or palimpsest, keep their shape while the small ones scatter around them.

A word ends at a space or at the end of a line, so a word the line breaks in two is two pieces.

III · By the line

Whole lines slide where the browser chose to break them, measured from the middle of each line.

Narrow the window: the lines run again, the layer reads them anew, and their centres follow without a word of code.

The same three centres serve any effect: turn a word about its middle, stagger the lines, or swell each letter in place.

Plate VI · Of the hand that confounds

The letters forget themselves.

Litterae sui obliviscuntur.

Drag the hand across the folio. Where its current runs, each letter loses its name and tries on others from the same fount, faster where the stroke is strong, then remembers as the trail fades.

Plate VII · Of the press itself

A thousand lines, one pull.

Mille lineae, una impressio.

Columns, justification, small capitals and the margin all follow the page. Scroll fast: the whole volume is a single draw.

What the font cannot draw, ✦ or 日本語, stays in the page entire, and the console says why.

Plate VIII · Of reading by candle

The night office.

Lux in tenebris lucet.

The chapel is dark and the book is open. Carry the candle with the hand: the letters are lit by it, as the page is, and fall back into the dark behind it.

At the third hour of the night the brothers rise and go down to the choir, each with his own small flame, and read by it the psalms that were written for that hour.

A candle shows only a hand's breadth of the page at a time. The rest of the book waits in the dark, and the eye follows the light down the column, one line and then the next.

So move the hand slowly, and read what it lights.
