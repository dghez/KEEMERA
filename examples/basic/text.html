Keemera

Text the browser lays out, drawn on the GPU straight from quadratic Bézier outlines: sharp at any size, kerned and wrapped exactly like the DOM.

Justified · text-transform · sup

Nothing here is laid out twice. The browser breaks the lines, applies kerning and letter-spacing, justifies the block and sets E = mc2; a Range per character reads each pen position back. The GPU only rasterises: one instanced draw for every element of a font pack and material, so scrolling moves one transform and a re-measure happens only when an element’s size, text or font changes. Ligatures are off on tracked elements, so “fi” and “fl” draw the same glyphs as the DOM.

Letter by letter

Every glyph is its own piece. The vertex shader moves each quad away from the pointer, around the center of the glyph’s ink.

Coverage is computed from the glyph’s own coordinates, so a moved quad still draws a sharp glyph, however far it travels.

The color follows the same force: a varying carries how far the glyph was pushed, and the fragment shader mixes toward the accent.

Move slowly along a line and the letters part like water, then settle back as the eased mouse leaves them behind.

Nothing here touches the CPU per frame: the store’s mouse and resolution are shared uniforms, read directly by the shader.

Word by word

Here every glyph of a word moves by the same amount, measured from the word’s center, so words travel as one piece.

Same buffers and same draw call as the letters, only a different center in the shader: keemeraWordCenter instead of the glyph’s.

Long words like synchronisation, typographically or straightforwardness hold together while the short ones scatter around them.

A word ends at a space or at a line break, so the two halves of a word that wraps would move as two pieces.

Colors shift word by word too: every glyph of a word shares one force, so a word changes color as a whole.

Line by line

And here whole lines slide, measured from each line’s center. The browser decides where the lines break.

Resize the window: the lines reflow, the layer measures them again, and their centers follow without any extra code.

Lines are found by comparing the glyph boxes the browser reports: a new line starts where a glyph stops overlapping the last one.

Hover near the middle of a paragraph and the lines above and below fan out, colored by how far each one moved.

The same three centers are there for any effect: rotate a word around its middle, stagger lines, or scale letters in place.
